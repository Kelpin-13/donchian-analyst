Gap-Up Rule Backtest

A rule that buys a large US stock after it opens at least 6% higher and holds that gain all day, then exits within days to weeks. It was tested on two separate lists of stocks, with its rules fixed before each test.

Summary

The rule

Market
Only when the S&P 500 (SPY) closed above its 200-day average the day before.
Stock
At least $50 million traded per day on average over 20 days, and at least one year of price history.
Signal
Today's open is at least 6% above yesterday's close, and today's close is at or above today's open. A gap this large in a large company usually follows news such as earnings or guidance.
Entry
Buy at today's close. The stop goes at today's low; skip the trade if that is more than 2 × the 14-day average daily range below the close.
Exit
Sell half at the close of the third day and move the stop to the entry price. Sell the rest at the next open after a close below the 10-day average. Stops fill at the stop or at the open if the day opens below it.
Size
0.5% of the account at risk per trade, at most 20% of the account in one stock, at most 10 positions, no borrowing. Idle cash earns the US Treasury bill rate.
Costs
0.05% per side on every buy and sell.

Out-of-sample tests

The pass rule was written before each test ran. It needs all three: better return per unit of drawdown than holding SPY; a higher annual return than 95% of 100 runs that buy random stocks from the same list on the same days with the same stops, exits and sizing; and a profit factor of at least 1.3 on at least 50 trades.

Against random entries

Our stock lists are today's large companies, so any rule that buys them looks good in hindsight (survivorship bias). The fair comparison is buying random stocks from the same list. Each bar counts the random runs that reached that annual return over the test period; the line is the rule.
